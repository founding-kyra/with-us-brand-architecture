Brand
Foundation

The Name

What With Us Means

With Us is not a brand name that was focus-grouped or designed to be clever. It came from something real. It reflects a way of moving through the world with your people beside you, not behind you. Not below you. The name carries a quiet but firm belief: that you go further when you go together, that the person next to you is part of the mission, not an afterthought.

In an industry full of brands that chase exclusivity through rejection, With Us takes a different stance. It builds walls the right way: not to keep people out, but to make the people inside feel like they genuinely belong to something.

You are not buying a shirt. You are joining a collective that was built for people who were told no and decided to build something better.

The Ethos

Rejection Created Obsession

This is not just a tagline. It is a founding truth. The people behind With Us know what it feels like to be overlooked, cut, passed over. They did not walk away from that. They walked toward something. That energy, that specific kind of focused hunger that only comes from being told you are not enough, is baked into the DNA of this brand.

It shows up in the details of the garments. It shows up in the way the brand chooses to grow. It shows up in the decision to make something premium not to exclude people but to fund a bigger vision for the communities they came from.

Every product that ships, every event that runs, every partnership that lands is proof of concept. Proof that rejection was the best thing that ever happened to them.

Alternate Tagline

Recommended Alternative: “Built by the Ones Who Stayed.”

The alternate tagline works differently. Where “Rejection Created Obsession” hits hard and specific, “Built by the Ones Who Stayed” speaks to endurance, to loyalty, to the kind of people who do not quit when it gets hard. It broadens the emotional entry point without losing the edge. It can live on product tags, on the website footer, in event collateral, anywhere the brand wants to land with weight but without aggression.

Both lines can coexist. Use “Rejection Created Obsession” as the brand’s origin statement, the thing that explains where they come from. Use “Built by the Ones Who Stayed” as the brand’s forward-facing identity, the thing that makes someone want to be part of it.

The brand does not chase. It attracts.

Brand Persona
& Voice

The Person

If With Us Were a Human Being

Picture someone who walks into a room and does not immediately fill it with noise. They are dressed right, not overdressed, just clearly someone who pays attention. They have been through things. You can tell without them saying a word. When they do speak, it is direct, warm, and specific. They are not performing confidence, they actually have it.

The brand persona draws from the archetype of Odell Beckham Jr.: an athlete who made fashion a natural extension of who he is rather than a costume. Someone who moves between the locker room and the front row without losing himself in either place. Style without trying. Community without performing. Standards without arrogance.

This is the human being the brand aspires to dress, to represent, and ultimately to become in the eyes of its customer.

The Feeling

What Interacting With This Brand Feels Like

The benchmark is a high-end watch boutique on a quiet afternoon. Not a mall store. Not a velvet rope. A place where someone who knows what they are doing is genuinely glad you came in, offers you something to sit down with, and talks to you like you have taste because they already assume you do.

That is the emotional temperature of With Us. Warm but elevated. Approachable but not cheap. There is no aggressive pitch, no manufactured urgency, no artificial scarcity countdown. The product earns the desire.

The brand does not chase. It attracts. And it does that by being exactly what it says it is, consistently, everywhere.

Voice & Tone

How With Us Speaks

The brand voice is grounded, specific, and human. It does not talk down to the customer and it does not try to be cool by being vague. It says what it means in the fewest words that still feel like something.

Short sentences carry more weight here than long ones. Plain language is preferred over industry speak. The brand never sounds like it is trying to impress anyone. It is already past that.

Market
Positioning

The White Space

Where With Us Lives in the Market

The performance apparel market has a gap that nobody has properly filled. On one side, you have luxury streetwear: Chrome Hearts, Rhude, Palm Angels. These brands have desirability in abundance. What they do not have is function. You cannot train in them. They are not built for movement. They exist for the look.

On the other side, you have performance-focused brands: Alo, Lululemon, Nike Tech. They have the function. They have the quality. But their aesthetic speaks to a customer who is going to yoga, not to a professional athlete who moves between high-level training and high-level social environments.

With Us sits directly in the gap between these two worlds. It is the only choice for the person who refuses to compromise. The athlete who wants to look premium off the field without changing before stepping onto it.

Not luxury that pretends to be functional. Not functional that tries to look good. Both things, built correctly, from the ground up.

The Pricing Philosophy

Nike vs. Shaq’s: Why Premium Pricing Has a Mission

The brand deliberately prices at the premium end. This is not ego. It is strategy with a conscience. The working principle is simple: build something so desirable and so well-made for the customer who can afford it that the margins fund the mission for the community that cannot.

Premium pricing builds aspiration in the market. It creates the kind of desire that trickles down organically, where a kid sees someone they respect wearing it and wants to earn it for themselves. Meanwhile, the revenue from those premium margins funds youth sponsorships, community clinics, and accessible gear for athletes who are on their way up but not there yet.

This is not charity. It is a business model with values built in from the start.

Audience

Primary Customer

The With Us Customer

The primary With Us customer is 18 to 32 years old. They are an athlete, a musician, an entertainer, or someone adjacent to those worlds. They train seriously. They have taste. They know the difference between something well-made and something that just looks well-made. They move between environments, from the training facility to the studio to the restaurant, and they do not want to change clothes to do it.

They are trend-conscious but not trend-chasing. They set the direction, they do not follow it. They have an opinion on Jerry Lorenzo’s last collection and they also have an opinion on proper compression fabric. Both things exist in the same person.

The Mark

Monogram & Wordmark

The With Us mark is a blackletter-inspired monogram built from the letters W and U, fused into a single form that reads as both initials and a unified symbol. It is deliberately heavy, referencing the weight of heritage type used in editorial mastheads and classic print, then stripped back to feel modern and quiet on a garment.

The mark works hardest at larger sizes, on the front of a fitted cap, embossed on a chest, or as a full-bleed graphic. At smaller sizes, the wordmark WITHUS in tracked capitals takes over, giving the brand a clean, confident secondary identity that reads clearly on tags, labels, and digital UI.

Logo Don'ts

What Never Happens to the Logo

✕ Never stretch or distort the mark in any direction.
✕ Never place the champagne logo on a light background. It only works on dark or photographed surfaces.
✕ Never place the dark logo on a black or near-black surface without sufficient contrast.
✕ Never add drop shadows, outlines, or effects to the logo.
✕ Never use the icon mark at sizes below 24px or 0.5 inches in print.
✕ Never recreate the logo in any other typeface or weight.
✕ Never surround the logo with other graphic elements that compete with its weight.

Product Philosophy

Simple on the Surface.
Serious Underneath.

The With Us product aesthetic is built on the belief that real quality does not need to announce itself. A garment should look clean and right from ten feet away. It should reveal its craft when someone gets close enough to actually touch it. The tonal puff print that only shows itself in the right light. The hidden pocket that sits flush without a single wrinkle. The compression layer that is not visible from the outside but performs like a professional jersey when the work starts.

This is not minimalism for its own sake. It is confidence. A brand that trusts its product enough to let the product speak.

Made in L.A.

Manufacturing as Brand Value

Every garment is made in Los Angeles. This is not a marketing line. It is a commitment that shapes how the brand operates, who it works with, and what it stands for. Local manufacturing means the people making the product are part of the same community the brand serves. It means quality control is close. It means the supply chain is accountable.

The manufacturing partner absorbs the cost of errors. That is a non-negotiable condition of the relationship. It signals mutual investment in getting it right, not just getting it shipped.

Growth & Business
Strategy

The Collaboration Model

Growing Through Alignment, Not Advertising

With Us does not plan to grow by buying attention. The model is built on earned attention through genuine alignment. The Kith playbook is the reference point: identify niche brands with established audiences, build collaborations that make sense culturally and aesthetically, and let the combined fanbase of both brands discover something new together.

Every collaboration is a proof of concept. It proves that With Us can hold its own beside established names. It proves that the product is good enough to anchor a joint release. And it introduces the brand to a new audience who came for the collaboration and stays for what With Us is building on its own.

Launch Strategy

How the Brand Enters the World

The launch is not a website going live. The launch is an event. Co-hosted with 88 Studios, the launch party is designed to be a moment that people feel, not just a product drop they scroll past. QR codes at the event connect the physical experience directly to the digital store, offering a 50% discount to everyone in the room. This is not a sale. It is an initiation.

The first 500 people who join the email list get one full year of exclusive benefits. That number is deliberate. It is small enough to feel real and large enough to build a genuine founding community.

The first drop is a soft launch: a handful of t-shirts and mesh shorts. Enough to test the supply chain, prove the quality, and plant the seed. The full collection follows when the infrastructure is proven.

Financial Horizon

Where This Goes

The target is $3 million to $5 million in revenue within three years. That is achievable at this price point with a properly seeded community, a collaboration pipeline, and seasonal drops that create consistent demand without oversaturating the market.

The revenue trajectory is not the point by itself. The revenue funds the mission. It funds the youth sponsorships, the community clinics, the accessible gear. Every dollar at the premium end of the price range is doing two jobs: building the brand above and investing in the community below.

Digital & Website
Strategy

The Experience

A Website That Earns Attention

The reference point is Aimé Leon Dore. Not because With Us is trying to be ALD, but because ALD understood something important: a website for a clothing brand is not a catalogue. It is a world. You enter it and you understand something about who the people behind the brand are and why they made what they made.

With Us adopts this principle completely. The site is cinematic. It moves with intention. Scrolling is not passive. As the visitor moves down the page, the site zooms into the garment details, the things you would only notice if you were holding the piece in your hands. Hidden pockets become visible. Puff print catches the light. The compression layer separates from the shell. The website does the tactile work that a physical store would do.
